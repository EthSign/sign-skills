# Sign slide layout patterns

Choose the smallest archetype that communicates the slide's single message. Do not combine multiple archetypes merely to fill space.

| Pattern | Choose it when | Composition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title slide | Opening a deck or presenting one decisive proposition | Full-bleed orange gradient or approved image treatment; centered 80 px display title, tracked subtitle, minimal footer |
| Section slide | Marking a true narrative transition | Full-bleed orange gradient or approved dark background; one 60 px statement and optional emphasized phrase |
| Content slide | Explaining one idea with text or a single visual | Standard header/footer; body frame contains one dominant object and limited support |
| Split panel + list | Contrasting a highlighted proposition with supporting evidence | Warm or orange highlight panel on one side; numbered or ruled list on the other |
| Feature cards | Comparing two or three parallel capabilities | Equal-height flat cards; short heading and restrained copy; avoid more than three primary cards |
| Track comparison | Comparing approaches, phases, or workstreams | One highlighted track, remaining tracks quieter; consistent columns and rules |
| Stat row | Presenting two or three defensible numbers | Large Archivo numerals, mono labels, ample empty space; cite figures in notes |
| Quote | Letting one authoritative statement carry the slide | Large pull quote with orange rule; attribution is secondary |
| Roadmap | Showing a short sequence with ownership or outcomes | Four or five steps on a clean baseline; mono phase labels and editable connectors |
| Data table | Exact comparison is more important than visual drama | Native editable table, sparse rules, orange reserved for header or one emphasized row |
| Agenda | Previewing a deliberate sequence | Numbered list with generous spacing; only the active or important item is orange |
| Team | Introducing a small leadership group | Consistent portraits and short roles; no decorative biography cards |

## Composition rules

- Lead with an editorial headline that states the point; avoid labels such as `Features`, `Architecture`, or `Dashboard` unless the slide truly is a reference page.
- One slide should have one dominant idea, one dominant visual, and at most one supporting region.
- Prefer whitespace, hairlines, and alignment over boxes. Avoid card soup.
- Use a single strong contrast: before/after, allowed/not allowed, current/future, or one highlighted track.
- Use diagrams only when relationships or sequence are harder to understand in prose. Keep diagrams editable and label states clearly.
- Use product screenshots at a legible crop. Frame them with one sentence telling the viewer what to notice; do not shrink a full interface into an unreadable thumbnail.
- Use a section slide only when the story changes phase. A short deck may need none after the cover.

## Demo and sales decks

For a 3–5 minute demo, target five or six slides and one memorable scenario. A useful arc is: possibility, current gap, governed mechanism, decisive product moment, evidence/outcome, invitation to pilot. Do not spend the main arc touring navigation or proving table-stakes functions.

Place the recorded product moment where it resolves the central tension. Use the surrounding slides to tell the audience what to notice and why it matters. Architecture, exhaustive features, and implementation detail usually belong in an appendix.

## Review rubric

Score 0–2 for each: activation precision, source handling, brand fidelity, layout judgment, asset integrity, scope preservation, and visual verification. Pass at 12/14 or higher with no hard failure.

Hard failures: changing factual claims without authorization; altering the logo; full-orange ordinary content slides; stale temporary Figma URLs; silent font substitution; or delivery without rendering and visual inspection.
